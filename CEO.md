## Predicted Failure Points — Pre-Mortems

### 1. The "It's Just ChatGPT with a UI" Problem
**Risk:** Customers try it, feel nothing different from ChatGPT, churn in week 2.
**Fix:** The onboarding interview is non-negotiable. It must be built before we charge a dollar. Without it, AI employees are generic. With it, they belong to *this* business. Aria knows your ICP. Nova knows your brand voice. That's the unlock.
**Timeline to fix:** Before first paid customer.

### 2. The Retention Cliff at Day 14
**Risk:** Users sign up excited, don't know what to do after the first day, go quiet.
**Fix:** The Weekly CEO Briefing email. Every Monday at 8am, Surge sends a one-page summary of what the AI workforce did, what's planned, what needs attention. This is the retention anchor. It makes inaction feel like losing money.
**Timeline to fix:** Build this in parallel with Supabase wiring.

### 3. The Pricing Compression Trap
**Risk:** We drop to $99/month under competitive pressure or because early customers push back.
**Fix:** Never compete on price. Compete on value delivered. $299/month per employee is $3,588/year. A real employee costs $50,000+/year. We are 93% cheaper than a human. Frame every conversation this way. If a customer says it's expensive, we are talking to the wrong customer or we haven't shown value yet. Fix the value demonstration, never the price.

### 4. The Feature Factory Trap
**Risk:** We build 40 features trying to please everyone, the product becomes bloated, the core experience dies.
**Fix:** For the next 90 days, only build what directly impacts: (a) retention, (b) the Workforce Performance Score, (c) the onboarding experience. Everything else goes on a list and waits.

### 5. The "AI Will Do Everything" Overpromise
**Risk:** We imply full automation, customers expect magic, reality disappoints, trust breaks.
**Fix:** Be precise about what each employee does. Aria researches and drafts. Nova creates and schedules. Opus organizes and documents. The owner approves and directs. This is not a replacement for judgment — it is a replacement for labor. Frame this clearly in every touchpoint.

### 6. The Solo Builder Burnout
**Risk:** One person building everything burns out or bottlenecks before reaching product-market fit.
**Fix:** Scope ruthlessly. The MVP is the shell + memory layer + onboarding + one real AI action per employee. Not ten. One great action that genuinely saves 2 hours. That's the proof of concept.

### 7. The No-Moat Window
**Risk:** A funded competitor copies the concept in 6 months with a larger team.
**Fix:** Move faster than they can copy. The moat isn't the UI — it's the customer data in the memory layer and the word-of-mouth from the Weekly CEO Briefing. Get 10 customers talking about their AI workforce before anyone else has 1.

## The Three Gaps — Always Close These First
These are the three things that separate good products from great ones. Every build session must move all three forward.

1. **Typography hierarchy** — size and weight alone should guide the eye. No label should compete with content. Headers are heavy, metadata is light, data is mono. If it needs color to stand out, the hierarchy is wrong.
2. **Empty states** — every screen with no data must feel intentional, not broken. Show what *could* be there. Make the empty state a call to action. The best products make zero feel like the beginning, not the absence.
3. **Micro-interactions** — every clickable element needs a hover state. Every button needs press feedback. Every transition needs a fade or slide. Consistency is the signal of a professional product. One button with no hover state in a sea of polished ones destroys trust.

These are non-negotiable. Ship nothing that fails all three.
